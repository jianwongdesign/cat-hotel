# Replacement direction study

Mechanism: owners can examine the actual room, compare capacity/rates and walk through a stay booking. Scene: a Singapore cat owner planning a trip on a phone or laptop. The homepage must show a cared-for place and make choosing a room immediate.

Rejected rut: sparse cream hotel brochure with oversized serif text, generous empty sections and repeated horizontal room rows. Predictable opposite: noisy cartoon pet shop with sticker badges. Neither meets the request.

Seven viable systems, in resonance order:
1. Shophouse guesthouse reception: warm plum, parchment and peach; generous rounded humanist lettering, immersive lounge and a substantial reservation desk joining the opening. Recognisable welcome and practical task together.
2. Independent pet magazine photo story: aubergine and butter; bold soft display with compact sans copy, varied large photographs and annotated room gallery. Personality through imagery rather than stickers.
3. Neighbourhood patisserie window: cherry, warm white and blush; expressive rounded type, broad colour fields, inset window-like photography. Welcoming and tactile; risk of reading as food if over-literal.
4. Contemporary interior showroom: espresso and pale clay; crisp geometric sans, room gallery with large images and anchored comparison. Clear actual accommodation; risk of coldness.
5. Cat-owner keepsake album: warm walnut, soft rose and natural white; friendly condensed headings, full-photo spreads and captions. Intimate real cats and owners' reassurance, without fake testimony.
6. Conservation-house courtyard: deep foliage, warm peach and ivory; robust sans, photo-led framed spaces and a stable bottom reservation rail. Singapore character; avoid invented architectural claims.
7. Travel journal field guide: terracotta, burgundy and warm white; compact humanist navigation, visual room index and a clear itinerary-like booking sequence. Useful browse-to-book rhythm; no fake passport/stamp decoration.
